BRET12 · CEREMA · Brest

What do French offshore wind farms really produce?

As we electrify transport, industry and heating, we will need far more clean electricity. Offshore wind is one of France's strongest cards — powerful, steady sea winds and the world's second-largest maritime area. Making the most of it starts with knowing what these farms actually generate.

The story

The gap between theory and reality

Today we rely on theoretical power curves — built from models and wind-tunnel tests on a single isolated turbine.

A real farm at sea behaves very differently. It is shaped by wake effects between turbines, wind direction, maintenance and more. Yet that gap between theory and reality is barely documented, while we plan and subsidise a multi-billion-euro industry on it.

For the first time, years of real hourly production from France's offshore farms are openly available. By crossing them with open wind data, we can reconstruct what these farms truly produce — and understand why reality drifts from the model.

The goal is an open, reproducible look at offshore wind performance: turning “we assume” into “we measured.”
01

The blind spot

Power curves come from models and wind-tunnel tests on one isolated turbine. They do not capture how a full farm behaves in the open sea.

02

The reality

Wakes, wind direction, maintenance and operations all shape output. The difference between forecast and reality is real, and it compounds at farm scale.

03

The mission

Combine open hourly production with open wind data to reconstruct real power curves and make offshore wind planning more honest.

Our approach

An open, reproducible workflow that moves from raw production records to a clearer picture of how French offshore wind farms really perform.

Source data

Built on open, hourly records

The project uses hourly production data from four French offshore wind farms: Saint-Nazaire, Saint-Brieuc, Fécamp and Yeu-Noirmoutier. The dataset spans 3 March 2023 to 1 October 2026.
